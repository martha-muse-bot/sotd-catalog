# Precedent Registry — SOTD Canonical Catalog

The graph of catalog decisions and the precedents they establish. Rules of the system
(user directive, 2026-10-21):

1. **Precedents bind.** Every discuss-queue presentation and every catalog opinion must
   cite the governing precedent(s) below. A recommendation that deviates from a standing
   precedent must say so explicitly and justify the deviation.
2. **Precedents can be overturned — by strong evidence, not by preference.** An overturn
   is recorded here (status changed, never silently edited) with the evidence that
   forced it.
3. **Overturns cascade.** When a precedent falls, every decision listed under its
   "Relied on by" is queued for re-review; those decisions stand until re-reviewed, but
   they are flagged, not trusted blindly.
4. **New rulings join the graph.** Each user ruling either relies on existing precedents
   (recorded under them) or establishes a new one (added here with its establishing
   decision).

Status key: STANDING · OVERTURNED · SUPERSEDED.

## P-01 — Community treatment defines product identity
**Statement:** Where the community consistently treats two things as different
products, they are separate entries, even with shared lineage or manufacture.
**Established by:** Polsilver vs Wizamet separation; Personna Med Prep kept separate
from Lab Blue (2026-10-20).
**Relied on by:** §A2 Personna Red kept separate from Platinum (2026-10-21);
§A6 Rex/Tatra (with P-04).
**Status:** STANDING.

## P-02 — Rename-in-place merges; canonical name is the maker's CURRENT name
**Statement:** When a maker renames a product in place, it is one entry; the current
name is canonical and the former name becomes an alias. (User rationale: a buyer of a
new tub today reports the name on the tub.) Product bases/eras are attributes.
**Established by:** §0b-1 B&M Spice / Reserve Spice (2026-10-21).
**Relied on by:** the whole Barrister's Reserve line application — Spice, Waves,
Cool, Fern, Lavender across soap/aftershave/fragrance (2026-10-21).
**Status:** STANDING.

## P-03 — Material, finish, and gap are attributes; geometry is identity
**Statement:** For razors, the same geometry in different materials, finishes, or
gaps is ONE entry with attributes (user example: Blackland Blackbird SS/Ti/polished).
A meaningfully different geometry is a different razor (user example: Timeless
Aluminum vs Timeless Aluminum Slant).
**Established by:** §0b-3 Timeless restructure (2026-10-21).
**Relied on by:** Timeless 4-entry structure (2026-10-21).
**Refined by:** P-17 (2026-10-28) — sequential revisions and variant dimensions of one named model are attributes, not entries; geometry-is-identity survives for distinct models and parallel designs.
**Status:** STANDING.

## P-04 — Brand of record / user intent is identity; manufacture is an attribute
**Statement:** Catalog identity follows the brand the user intends — the brand of
record — because a brand owner can move production between manufacturers and the
product-as-bought stays the same. Manufacture and lineage are recorded as
attributes/notes, never as identity.
**Established by:** §A4 Parker Injector (AccuTec-made, Parker entry) and §A6
Rex/Tatra (Czech Blades manufacture in notes), both 2026-10-21, plus the user's
stated rationale.
**Relied on by:** §A8 Crystal (brand Crystal, ASR manufacture noted); §A3-REV Ted
Pella (see Overturned, below).
**Status:** STANDING.

## P-05 — A maker's own channel/co-brand name merges as an alternate name
**Statement:** When the second name is the maker's (or its distributor's) own
channel brand for the identical product — evidenced by a dual-branded listing — the
names merge: one entry, second name as alternate. Boundary with P-04: an independent
brand sourcing manufacture is a separate entry (P-04); a co-brand of the same
maker's product merges (P-05).
**Established by:** Personna Lab Blue = AccuTec AccuForge (2026-10-20).
**Relied on by:** §A1 Fromm hair shaper → Personna Hair Shaper (SalonCentric's
dual-branded `fromm-personna-…` listing), 2026-10-21.
**Status:** STANDING.

## P-06 — Packaging variant is not identity
**Statement:** The same product sold in different packaging (tuck vs dispenser,
with/without blade bank) is one entry; packaging is a variant attribute.
**Established by:** §A5 Dorco ST301 merged into ST300 (2026-10-21).
**Relied on by:** —
**Status:** STANDING.

## P-07 — Bare-name popularity default
**Statement:** When a bare name could be two variants and one is clearly more
popular, the bare entry defaults to the popular one (bare "Astra" = Superior
Platinum green; bare "Minora" = Minora Stainless; bare "Wilkinson Sword" =
Edgewell Classic unless the post says Gillette; bare "Feather" in DE context =
Hi-Stainless; bare "Schick" resolves per the razor named).
**Established by:** standing convention (2026-10-20).
**Relied on by:** bare "Timeless" → standard-head entry (2026-10-21); bare "Dorco
Stainless" → ST300 line (§A5); §B6 "Feather Pro" → AC Professional (2026-10-21;
naming-behavior asymmetry — Pro Super users say "Super" — reinforced the
volume default).
**Application note (2026-10-21):** P-07 survives as the leader-selection rule
only — its outputs are applied as P-15 loose aliases per the universal ruling
(see P-15, "UNIVERSAL APPLICATION").
**Status:** STANDING.

## P-08 — Set cross-validation is bidirectional, verified siblings only
**Statement:** Soap/aftershave/fragrance sharing maker+scent form a set; validating
any member on primary evidence validates all unverified siblings. Attested or
corroborated siblings do NOT unlock a set.
**Established by:** user rule (2026-10-20).
**Relied on by:** Lane 1 harvest promotions; §0b-4 Cerberus set_key treatment.
**Status:** STANDING.

## P-09 — Collaboration attribution is per-format maker
**Statement:** For a multi-house collaboration, each format's entry sits under the
house that made that format, with the other houses cross-noted; the set shares one
set_key.
**Established by:** §0b-4 Cerberus (soap = Declaration Grooming, aftershave = Noble
Otter, fragrance = House of Mammoth), 2026-10-21 — overruling the aligned team
recommendation of a single primary house.
**Relied on by:** —
**Status:** STANDING.

## P-10 — A purchase-time option is an attribute, not a product
**Statement:** An option selected at purchase on a single product listing (e.g. an
add-on checkbox) does not create a separate entry.
**Established by:** §0b-5 Stirling Unscented-with-Menthol fold (2026-10-21).
**Relied on by:** —

## P-11 — Unresolved identity reports as its own line
**Statement:** An identity-disputed cluster is neither silently absorbed nor
promoted: it keeps its own entry and its own counts at its own tier, with a
candidate-alias record naming the proposed fold target and the dispute. It cannot
be attested until the dispute resolves.
**Established by:** §A7 Rapira Stainless Chrome parked against Super Stainless
(2026-10-21).
**Relied on by:** —
**Status:** STANDING.

## P-15 — Loose alias: probabilistic default, flagged, share recorded
**Statement:** When a string is ambiguous among KNOWN, REAL products and carries
no discriminating signal, it may resolve to the most-used candidate as a LOOSE
alias: the match is flagged probabilistic (never presented as certain), and the
alias records its basis — the leader's share among identified uses of that
family. No dominance floor: the recorded share is the disclosure. Reports show
loose-alias counts as a distinct probable sub-line, and the assignment unwinds
if real evidence arrives. Loose aliases never settle IDENTITY disputes (whether
a distinct product exists — that stays P-11 unresolved).
**Established by:** user ruling on §B7 "Shark (Egypt)" (2026-10-21) — his
argument: bare "Astra" → SP rests on base rates alone, and "(Egypt)" is the
same shape of problem; the binary alias-or-nothing treatment was inconsistent.
**UNIVERSAL APPLICATION (user, 2026-10-21):** ALL likelihood-based defaults
are loose — including the former P-07 popularity defaults (bare "Astra" → SP
first among them: "We aren't confident they used Astra SP, it's just very
very likely"). P-07 survives only as the leader-selection rule; its outputs
are applied as P-15 loose aliases. What stays HARD: resolutions where the
string's own semantics pick exactly one candidate (§B5 "Perma-Sharp
Stainless" — the brand's only DE blade; "Shark Stainless" — the line's only
stainless-named product), per-post razor-format resolution (P-14), and
proven author-scoped aliases (§B4).
**Boundary (user, 2026-10-21, §B4):** the premise is NO discriminating signal —
and a conspicuously OMITTED discriminator is signal. Posters who mean Gillette
Silver Blue write the full name or "GSB"; a bare "Gillette Blue" from an
unproven author drops the key word and points away from Silver Blue, so the
§B4 residue does NOT loose-alias (it stays ambiguous). Base rates computed
from full-name writers do not transfer to a self-selected shorthand
population whose shorthand omits the discriminator.
**Relied on by:** §B7 "Shark (Egypt)" → Shark Super Stainless (share computed
from identified Shark uses); §B7 bare "Shark" → Shark Super Stainless (2026-10-28; same family basis, share 0.430408; Chrome leads identified uses at 0.461 — rests on nmrs CM + volume lore + consistency, not leadership); §B7 bare "Wilkinson Sword" + bare "Wilkinson" → Wilkinson Sword Classic (2026-10-28; share 0.165626, Classic 398 vs Gillette 2,005 — convention + §B4, not leadership; required matcher fix for loose to outrank synthesized keys); §B8 bare "ASCO" → Super Stainless (2026-10-21);
§B9 bare "Gillette Winner" → Winner Platinum (2026-10-21); bare "Feather"
in an AC-razor post → AC Professional (2026-10-21; within-format loose alias
under the P-14 hard constraint — the Professional is the lineup's standard
per Feather's distributor Jatai, ProGuard second).
**Status:** STANDING.

## P-14 — Razor–blade format agreement disambiguates first
**Statement:** Blade and format must agree within a post. When a blade string is
ambiguous across formats or products, the format of the razor named in the same
post decides (DE razor → the DE blade; AC razor → the AC blade) — and vice versa
when a razor name is the ambiguous one. This check runs BEFORE the popularity
default (P-07), which applies only when the pairing gives no signal.
**Established by:** user ruling on §B1 bare "Kai" (2026-10-21) — no blanket
alias to the DE entry; per-post razor format decides.
**HARD CONSTRAINT (user, 2026-10-21):** no default — popularity, loose, or
otherwise — may ever resolve a blade AGAINST the paired razor's format. Bare
"Feather" in an AC-razor post defaulting to the DE Hi-Stainless is the named
violation ("in no case"). When the razor's format is known and no
within-format pick is defensible, the string stays ambiguous WITHIN that
format (or loose-aliases within it, per P-15) — it never crosses formats.
**Relied on by:** §B1 bare "Kai"; §B3 bare "Personna" (razor cross-check, as the
team had proposed); generalizes the earlier per-post Schick decision (B2).
**Status:** STANDING.

## P-13 — Every tier is visible in matching and reporting
**Statement:** The matcher indexes every catalog tier. Resolution precedence:
verified (and needs-review) > attested/corroborated (their own middle tier) >
unverified clusters. A product must never disappear from usage reports because
its evidence improved; reports label each line with its tier.
**Established by:** matcher-extension ruling (2026-10-21), prompted by the
Crystal §A8 consolidation (attested entry resolving zero posts) and the Rubie
§A9 flag — ~7,800 attested/corroborated entries were matcher-invisible.
**Relied on by:** all coverage/usage reporting from 2026-10-21 on.
**Status:** STANDING.

## P-12 — Indistinguishable strings share one entry (no false precision)
**Statement:** When posters' strings cannot distinguish two label variants, they
share one entry with both variants recorded; splitting would manufacture counts
that are guesses dressed as data.
**Established by:** §A9 Gillette Rubie / Rubie Platinum Plus (2026-10-21).
**Relied on by:** §A11 Van der Hagen — one entry recording both real variants
(Basics/China and Ice Tempered/Germany) because bare strings cannot be sorted
between them (2026-10-21).
**Status:** STANDING.

## P-16 — Fragrance-maker × soap-maker collab: brand of record follows label and retail presentation
**Statement:** When a fragrance house and a soap maker collaborate, brand of
record follows how the product was labeled and sold, not who poured it. The
soap carries JOINT collab credit with the fragrance house primary (the maker
as manufacture attribute); the aftershave/fragrance line stays the fragrance
house's alone. Consequence for set cross-validation: same-scent soap +
aftershave pairs are NEVER same-maker sets in this pattern — cross-brand by
design — even when the same soap maker also made the scented aftershave (the
set rule propagates within one brand's line, and the aftershave line is the
fragrance house's).
**Established by:** Chatillon Lux × Declaration Grooming ruling (2026-10-22,
user). Evidence: DG sold the soaps on declarationgrooming.com (CL's site never
listed them; CL's June 2020 announcement made DG "sole provider of Chatillon
Lux fragrances in shaving product bases"); tubs carried DG's milksteak-era
label artwork with the Chatillon Lux name prominent; CL's own copy: "soap in
the Icarus base from Declaration Grooming, as well as *our own* aftershave and
toner". Dataset 2018–2026: 84% of CL soap posts name both brands (82% of those
CL-first); aftershave posts are 100% "Chatillon Lux" alone.
**Relied on by:** Chatillon Lux soap/aftershave entries; the standing
cross-brand set-exclusion note in the knowledge base; Cerberus entries
(soap/aftershaves/fragrance first re-attributed HoM-primary 2026-10-22, then
REVISED to label/retail-brand primary — DG×HoM / NO×HoM with HoM as scent
creator — same day after EdP label photos showed DG-only branding).
**Status:** STANDING.

## P-17 — Model entries carry variant dimensions as attributes
**Statement:** A maker's named model is ONE entry. Sequential revisions of
that model (AlumiGoose V1→V2), and its eras, handles, finishes, countries,
and base-plate variants, are attributes of the model entry — recorded as
variant aliases on the entry — not separate entries. This preserves
model-level popularity counts instead of diluting them across versions, and
avoids manufacturing version precision the posts don't support (99% of
AlumiGoose posts are bare). Separate entries only for distinct model names,
distinct makers, and distinct designs sold in parallel.
**Established by:** Mongoose/AlumiGoose ruling (2026-10-28, user) +
Gillette Tech analysis (2026-10-28).
**Refines:** P-03 — P-03's geometry-is-identity rule survives for distinct
models and parallel designs (Timeless Slant, WR1 vs WR2); it no longer splits
sequential revisions or variant dimensions of one named model.
**Relied on by:** Mongoose 2-entry structure (razor/mongoose-mongoose,
razor/mongoose-alumigoose, 2026-10-28); Tech attribute-fold project (queued
2026-10-28).
**Open boundary:** Ranger Tech / Milord — sold under distinct model names
but Tech-based; user call still owed. Other makers' Tech-styles (Baili,
RazoRock, iKon) stay separate entries.
**Status:** STANDING.

## P-18 — Upstream confidence implies own-catalog application
**Statement:** A catalog correction proposed to nmrs at 100% confidence
(the PR bar) is applied to our own catalog in the same turn. If we are
confident enough to assert a mapping upstream, withholding it from
ourselves is incoherent.
**Scope:** Catalog corrections only (correct_matches / catalog YAML).
Code fixes to nmrs (e.g. PR #4's override-manager fix) have no
own-catalog counterpart — our matcher is separate code — and are excluded.
**Escape hatch:** If our own dataset evidence contradicts the upstream
mapping (our posts show the string means something else here), hold for
joint review instead of applying blindly. The contradiction, not the
quota, decides.
**Established by:** Yusuf ruling (2026-10-28, user) — PR #3's DG
Yusuf→Yuzu mapping applied to our catalog the same day; the WK/CL Yusuf
string followed from the user's 2026-10-27 typo ruling + the string's own
maker tag, not from PR #3 (PR #3 covered the DG string only).
**Status:** STANDING.

## P-19 — Single-owner custom commissions are not entries
**Statement:** A custom-commissioned item made for one owner (one physical
item, one poster) is not a catalog entry, however many times that owner
posts it. Its strings resolve at brand level.
**Established by:** Böker §6 ruling (2026-10-28, user) — Marquis90's
custom-designed Böker 6/8 (~283 posts, one razor).
**Contrast:** Koraat §5 named designs WERE cataloged — they recur across
owners and community runs. Single-owner + single-item fails that test.
**Status:** STANDING.

## P-20 — Clone buckets: specific per cloned model, generic per format
**Statement:** Clones of a named model with a coherent multi-author family get a
specific bucket entry (brand Other, never under the cloned maker); other known
clone strings go to generic per-format fallback buckets (Other DE/AC/Injector
Clone). Buckets carry a blade-format type so P-14 agreement still works. A
single-author clone term goes generic, not specific. Maker-named clone products
are entries, not buckets. Multi-razor/essay strings stay clustered (P-11).
**Established by:** Clone-bucket design ruling (2026-10-28, user tap: specific +
generic fallback) — first application: Kai Captain + Kai Kamisori Clone (88/4),
Futur Clone (226/43), generic DE (92/37), AC (177/9), Injector (27/2, tier
unverified on the 2-author basis).
**Contrast:** P-19 (single-owner commissions are not entries) — clones are
multi-owner by nature, so they get buckets, not brand-level resolution.
**Status:** STANDING.

## P-21 — Version attribution: version strings certain, author pin outranks era
**Statement:** For a versioned product line (e.g. Claymore V1/V2/V3/Evolution),
version-specific strings resolve to their version, certain. Bare (unversioned)
strings resolve by author pin first, then maker, then era — never by
latest-model or base-model default, and never by per-post era splitting.
An author pin governs ALL of that author's bare strings for the product:
an author's version-specific string pins their bare strings before and
after it (same physical razor); an author's first bare post, resolved by
maker or era, pins their later bare strings, which are NOT re-resolved by
their own years. Confidence propagates from the pin and never upgrades:
an explicit version pin makes later bare strings certain by author scope
(P-15 hard carve-out); a loose era/maker pin keeps later bare strings
loose. An unpinned author's first bare post resolves by maker (certain
only when the maker uniquely pins the version, e.g. Alpha → Evolution),
then era (loose). What remains unresolved goes to a loose era leader
with its share recorded, or to a P-11 ambiguous line. An author with two
version pins (owns two versions) has later bare strings ambiguous between
their own pins unless context disambiguates. The rule is
backward-protective: re-runs must preserve pins, never silently era-split
an author's history when a new version appears.
**Established by:** Claymore V2/V3 attribution ruling (2026-10-28, user
tap: record it with author continuity) — user observed that an author
posting bare "Claymore" across years means the same razor, not a new
purchase per era.
**Contrast:** P-17 (model attributes) — versions that differ in geometry
are distinct models (Claymore V1 curved vs V2 angled head, P-03), so
version attribution is about WHICH model a bare string names, not about
folding versions as attributes. P-15 (loose defaults) — era defaults here
are loose and era-bounded, never certain.
**Matcher implication (future build):** needs an author-pin table consulted
before era defaults, plus era windows on loose aliases; neither exists
yet, and no build is owed until V2/V3 strings actually appear (zero in
the dataset as of 2026-10-28).
**Status:** STANDING.

## P-22 — Soap base is an attribute, never identity
**Statement:** A soap's base (tallow, vegan, Donkeybase, Maxima, Optimus,
and the like) is an attribute of the scent entry, never its identity.
Base alone never splits a soap entry: one maker + one scent = one entry
within the soap category, with base transitions recorded as eras when a
post specifies them. Bare posts match the entry regardless of base.
Applies uniformly across brands; no brand-specific base treatment.
**Established by:** Aion base discussion (2026-10-28, user-approved) —
user observed that splitting by base breaks comparison of a long-lived
scent across bases and leaves most posts unassignable, since users
rarely name a base.
**Boundary:** A difference in name or scent is a different product
regardless of base. Barrister and Mann Roam vs Roam II stay separate:
Roam II (June 2024 limited edition, vegan base) is a different scent
profile — refined gentleman-cowboy, without the smoke-forward
true-to-trail nature of original Roam — not Roam in a new base.
Base-as-attribute folds only records differing by base and nothing
else (e.g. Roam Excelsior vs Roam Glissant).
**Contrast:** P-17 (model attributes) — same logic extended from models
to soap scents: revisions and eras are attributes of one entry. P-03
(material as attribute) — same principle: a variant dimension is not
identity. B&M Spice (§0b-1) — base already treated as an era attribute
there; P-22 generalizes it.
**Matcher implication:** Entries keyed by maker + scent; base recorded
as an era attribute when specified. Existing base-differentiated
records (e.g. B&M Roam Excelsior vs Glissant, both corroborated) fold
into single scent entries under this rule. Entry ids embedding a base
(e.g. soap/aion-skincare-optimus-shaving-soap-reverence) want renames
to the base-free form at application.
**Status:** STANDING.

## P-23 — Anecdotal tier: real but currently unvalidatable products
**Statement:** Products that are real — credible poster testimony of
existence and use — but unvalidatable *at this time* (primary evidence
cannot exist yet in principle, not merely hard to find) get the entry
status `anecdotal`, below corroborated. The loop spends no further
research slots on them — revisiting is futile *until something changes*.
The door stays open by design: if genuine primary evidence surfaces
later (the prototype goes into production, a maker page appears), the
entry promotes normally; nothing about the tier blocks that. The entry
persists so its strings keep matching (matching doesn't require
validatability), but anecdotal entries are never published in the
encyclopedia.
**Qualifying test:** (a) credible poster testimony that the thing existed
and was used, AND (b) a positive indication it was never retailed / no
listing could exist — typically the poster's own description as
prototype, one-off, test batch, or "never-born," plus no retail trace.
"We searched and found nothing" alone stays unverified; the positive
unvalidatability finding is what qualifies.
**Established by:** Baybarry discussion (2026-10-30, user-approved) —
Green Mountain × Savannah Bee prototype c.2016 ("il sapone mai nato"),
15 uses / 1 author, never retailed. User observed the dataset holds a
real category of prototypes, one-offs, and experiments and proposed the
terminal "anecdotal" state; the shape (terminal, still matches, never
published, below corroborated) was agreed in discussion.
**Boundary:** A sample *of* a real product (e.g. HoM avocado sample,
Meißner Tremonia sample) folds to the product entry — the unvalidatable
thing must be the entity itself. Multi-author prototypes (artisan test
batches sent to several people) qualify the same way; author count is
irrelevant, unvalidatability is what matters. The open door is
deliberate, not an afterthought: a prototype that later goes into
production (cf. Wolfman Guerrilla) promotes out of anecdotal normally —
"leave open" is a matter of permission (nothing blocks promotion), not
monitoring (nothing watches for it); the realistic reopening paths are
opportunistic enrichment or a human noticing.
**Contrast:** P-19 (custom commissions → not entries, resolve at brand
level) — anecdotal keeps the entry for matching fidelity instead of
dissolving it; a named prototype resolving to an anecdotal entry beats a
generic brand-level match. not_a_product — reserved for strings
referring to nothing real (jokes, misparses); anecdotal is for things
believed real.
**Matcher implication:** `anecdotal` added to MID_STATUSES in
match_all.py (2026-10-30) so anecdotal entries keep matching, ranking
below verified and alongside attested/corroborated, above unverified.
**Status:** STANDING.
**Applied:** 2026-10-30 — `unverified/soap/green-mountain-baybarry-savannah-bee-prototype-2016`
→ anecdotal; Q007314 resolved.

## P-24 — Own-thing substances: a distinct post-shave treatment is not an aftershave
**Statement:** A substance posters use as its own distinct post-shave step — a mineral-salt block/stick applied and rinsed off, antiseptic/astringent — is its own thing, not an aftershave. Bare strings for it stay on their own attested entry; they never fold into a branded product of that substance, and the substance is never deleted as a non-product. Branded products (Osma is one brand among several — Parker, Proraso, Omega also make alum blocks) are entries of their own, not merge targets for the bare string.
**Established by:** User ruling 2026-11-15 (Q000980): "Alum is not an aftershave. It's its own thing. Osma is one popular brand of Alum but there are several." Source: TRC alum-block guide (potassium/aluminum sulfate, antiseptic + astringent, applied after the shave, rinsed off).
**Boundary:** This is about identity, not the dataset field — bare "Alum" strings arrive in the aftershave field and the entry stays matchable there; the ruling does not create a new catalog category by itself. Whether alum (and its branded blocks) gets its own category is a separate structural decision, still open.
**Contrast:** P-20 (generic buckets for non-products) — Alum is not a bucket for clones; it is a real substance with its own use. Witch-hazel exclusion at build (ingredient/additive) does not extend to a substance posters use as a standalone step.
**Status:** STANDING.
**Applied:** 2026-11-15 — Q000980 resolved; `unverified/aftershave/alum` annotated, stays attested.

## P-25 — Composite split: a field string joining two or more products splits so each half matches its own entry
**Statement:** When one field string joins two or more products — semicolon-joined ("Alum block; B&M Paganini's Violin"), plus-joined ("Wolfman WR1-OC + WRH7"), or equivalent — the string is split at ingest/matcher level and each half matches its own entry. Composite clusters are not entries, not folds, and not deletions: they exist only until the split routes their rows, then retire. A half that matches nothing does not split off; the string stays whole and keeps matching its own cluster.
**Established by:** User ruling 2026-11-16 (Q006543, tap "Split at ingest/matcher level"), scope extended same day by the user: all fields, including multi-razor posts ("Rockwell 6S - R3 plate ; Fine Slant touchup", "ZY 6/8; Wüsthof Trident Barber's King 6/8").
**Scope measured 2026-11-16:** 13,183 semicolon-join rows across fields (aftershave 4,560; brush 3,152; soap 2,906; razor 1,432; fragrance 1,003; blade 130); alum-led semicolon aftershave class 48 clusters / 422 uses. Plus-join rows measured same day: 7,503 total (aftershave 3,781; brush 1,354; soap 1,293; razor 959; blade 46; fragrance 70) — noisier than ';' ("barrister + mann" is a brand-name spelling, not a join), so the match-guard carries more weight for '+'.
**Design (as specified to the user 2026-11-16):** split at match time in match_all.py, dataset rows untouched; separators ';' and '+' only — never 'and'/'&', which occur inside brand names (Barrister & Mann, Wade and Butcher, "B&M" itself); guard that a part splits off only if it matches a real entry, so prose halves ("...it lathers well; it's slick...") never split; counting: each matched half counts as one use of its product.
